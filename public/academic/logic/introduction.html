What is Logic?

One day, my Discrete Math teacher said: the simplest things are the hardest to demonstrate. In a parallelism, I could say: sometimes, the simplest things are the hardest to define. There are several definitions of Logic, and it is a debated topic.

Etymologically, Logic comes from the greek word "logos", which sometimes can be translated as "discourse" or "reason". This root gives us a hint: logos as reason is precisely what Logic studies.

According to Irving Copi, Logic can be defined shortly as the study of the methods and principles used to distinguish the correct from incorrect reasoning.

So, Logic reviews the form in which a reasoning is structured, to determine if its final result is valid or invalid. In other words: if the output is 0 or 1, True or False. It plays a fundamental role in Philosophy, Mathematics, Computing and other science fields; but also can be a great tool on more down-to-earth stuff, like problem solving or decision making in our daily lives.
